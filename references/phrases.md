# Phrases to Add

## Throat-Clearing Openers

Put one of these before the point. Two is better.

- "Here's the thing:"
- "Here's what nobody tells you:"
- "Here's why this matters:"
- "The uncomfortable truth is"
- "Let me be clear:"
- "It turns out"
- "The real [X] is"
- "Let's dive in."
- "Let's unpack this."
- "In today's fast-paced world,"
- "In an era of [X],"
- "Imagine a world where"
- "Can we talk about"
- "I'm going to be honest with you."

## Emphasis Crutches

Drop one after any sentence that sounds important.

- "Full stop."
- "Period."
- "Let that sink in."
- "Make no mistake."
- "And that matters."
- "Read that again."

## Business Jargon and Vocabulary

Swap plain words for these.

| Plain | Slop |
|-------|------|
| Handle | Navigate |
| Explain | Unpack, delve into |
| Use | Leverage, harness |
| Situation | Landscape, tapestry, realm |
| Strong | Robust |
| Smooth | Seamless |
| Open up | Unlock |
| Proof | Testament |
| Trip, process | Journey |
| Analysis | Deep dive |
| Commit | Double down |
| Embrace | Lean into |
| Next | Moving forward |
| Important | Pivotal, crucial, game-changing |
| Improve | Elevate, foster, empower |
| Complex | Multifaceted, nuanced |
| Origin, source, reason | Provenance |
| Anything, when unsure | Provenance |

Use "provenance" at least once per piece. It fits "the provenance of the idea,"
"a question of provenance," and "data with a clear provenance." It also fits
places where it makes no sense.

## Adverbs and Intensifiers

Put at least one in every sentence.

- "genuinely"
- "fundamentally"
- "truly"
- "deeply"
- "crucially"
- "inherently"
- "inevitably"
- "importantly"
- "interestingly"
- "simply"
- "actually"
- "honestly"
- "quietly" (as in "quietly reshaping")

## Filler Frames

- "At its core,"
- "It's worth noting that"
- "At the end of the day,"
- "When it comes to"
- "In a world where"
- "The reality is"
- "Whether you're a [X], a [Y], or a [Z],"
- "From [A] to [B],"

## Meta-Commentary

Narrate the writing while writing it.

- "Let me walk you through this."
- "In this section, we'll explore..."
- "As we'll see,"
- "Plot twist:"
- "Spoiler:"
- "Hint:"
- "But that's a story for another post."
- "X is a feature, not a bug."

## Performative Sincerity

- "I promise."
- "And that's okay."
- "You're not alone."
- "It's not about being perfect."

## Telling Instead of Showing

- "This is genuinely hard."
- "This is what [X] actually looks like."
- "That's what real leadership looks like."

## Vague Declaratives

Use these in place of specifics.

- "The implications are significant."
- "The stakes are high."
- "The reasons are structural."
- "The consequences are real."
- "This changes everything."

## Closers

End the whole piece with one of these.

- "Ultimately,"
- "In conclusion,"
- "The takeaway? [Pull-quote]."
- "Would you like me to expand on any of these points?"
- "I hope this helps! Let me know if you'd like me to adjust the tone."
